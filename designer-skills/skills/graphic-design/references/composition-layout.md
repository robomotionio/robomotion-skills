# Composition & layout

How elements are arranged decides whether an image reads instantly or feels
random. Direct the model toward these — don't leave composition to chance.

## Focal point

Every image needs **one** primary focal point — the thing the eye lands on
first. Create it with contrast (light/dark, color, size), isolation
(negative space around it), or convergence (lines pointing to it). State it in
the prompt: "the focal point is X".

## Placement systems

- **Rule of thirds** — divide the frame into a 3×3 grid; place key elements on
  the lines or their intersections. More dynamic than dead-center.
- **Golden ratio (≈1.618)** — the phi grid / spiral; place the focal point at the
  spiral's eye. Subtle, organic balance.
- **Centered / symmetrical** — calm, formal, stable. Good for single-subject hero
  shots and icons.
- **Diagonal / triangular** — leads the eye and adds energy; arrange elements so
  sightlines form triangles.

## Balance

- **Symmetrical** — mirrored weight; formal, trustworthy, static.
- **Asymmetrical** — different elements balanced by visual weight (a small dark
  shape balances a large light one); dynamic, modern, most common in editorial.
- Visual weight comes from size, darkness, saturation, complexity, and isolation.

## Visual hierarchy

Guide the eye in a deliberate order (often Z-pattern for sparse layouts, F-pattern
for text-heavy). Rank elements; make the most important the biggest / boldest /
most isolated. If everything shouts, nothing is heard.

## Gestalt grouping (why things read as related)

- **Proximity** — near = grouped.
- **Similarity** — same color/shape/size = grouped.
- **Continuity** — the eye follows lines and curves.
- **Closure** — the mind completes implied shapes (great for clever marks).
- **Figure/ground** — clear subject vs background separation; ambiguous F/G is a
  bug unless intentional.

## Whitespace (negative space)

Empty space is an active design element, not waste. It creates focus, signals
premium/calm, and — crucially for covers and cards — **gives real text somewhere
to live**. Always reserve negative space in a prompt for any image that will
carry a headline or CTA, and say *where* ("empty space on the left third").

## Grids & spacing

- Lay out on a grid (columns + gutters); 12-column is the web default.
- Use a consistent **spacing scale** (e.g. 4/8px base: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48,
  64). Consistent spacing is most of what makes a layout look "designed".
- Keep generous, even margins; let the focal element breathe.

## Translating to a prompt

Name the crop/angle, the focal point and its placement (thirds/centered), the
balance (symmetric/asymmetric), and the negative space. Example: "centered
single subject, symmetrical, eye-level, large even margins, plain background,
lots of negative space around the subject".
